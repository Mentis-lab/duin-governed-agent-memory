# Solar Shroud Radiation Shield

**Proposed by:** [[Jonas Wexley]] · **Status:** proposed for v1.1, not in rev 2

## The idea
Merge the solar panel mount and the radiation shield into one printed part. The panel sits on top, tilted, and its underside becomes the top plate of the stacked-plate shield. The panel shades the sensor, the shield plates carry the panel, and the kit loses a bracket, four screws and one assembly step.

## Why it matters
- One fewer part in a kit that outsiders assemble from a guide. Every removed step is one fewer thing [[Anouk Ferreira]] has to photograph and one fewer way a beta member can get it wrong.
- The panel is the largest flat surface on the station and currently does nothing for the sensor.
- Cost: roughly $3 less per kit in hardware and about four minutes less assembly, by Jonas's estimate.

## Why not in rev 2
[[2026-06-04-enclosure-redesign]] is already late. Jonas raised this on 2026-07-09 and [[Tessa Varga]] asked him to finish the shield that is proven before inventing a new one. He agreed. The stacked-plate design he measured on the Brightwater rooftop in July brought the solar bias down from +2.5 °C to +0.4 °C, and that number is what the [[Frost Alert for Orchards]] idea depends on. Changing the geometry now would mean measuring it again.

## Open questions
- Does a warm panel above the top plate heat the air column? [[Passive Radiation Shields for Low-Cost Stations]] says the top plate matters most, so this needs a test, not an opinion.
- Panel tilt is latitude-dependent; a merged part fixes the tilt. Two variants, or a hinge?
- Would it constrain the antenna position that rev 2 finally settled?

## Next step
Print one after rev 2 ships, mount it next to a rev 2 station on the rooftop, and compare a month of readings. Owner Jonas, earliest 2026-11.
