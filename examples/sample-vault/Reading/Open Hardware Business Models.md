# Reading: Open Hardware Business Models

**Source:** a series of essays by the founder of a small open-hardware studio on what actually pays the bills, read by [[Tessa Varga]] and [[Anouk Ferreira]] ahead of the license decision · **Read:** 2026-03-17

## What it says
The series argues that open hardware studios rarely make money on the design, and that pretending otherwise leads to bad licenses and worse products. The models that survive, in the author's experience:

1. **Kits.** People pay for parts sourced, tested and packed together, plus the confidence that they will work. The design being free does not reduce this; it increases the number of people who want the kit.
2. **Grants then kits.** A grant pays for the design and the documentation; kits pay for the studio afterwards. Fragile if the grant ends before the kits sell.
3. **Services.** Calibration, support contracts, custom variants for institutions. Steady but slow to build.
4. **Consulting off the reputation.** Common, and the thing that quietly eats the product.

On licenses: the author's view is that a reciprocal hardware license does not stop a determined cloner, but it does stop a lazy one, and lazy cloners are most of them. Permissive software licenses widen the community. Strong reciprocal licenses on the app tend to keep integrators away.

## What we took from it
- The license split in [[2026-03-19-open-source-license]] is this essay's recommendation almost exactly: reciprocal hardware, permissive software.
- Kestrel is model 2 right now. [[Corinne Abelard]] said the same thing in different words at [[2026-04-02-lodestar-grant-call]]. The risk is the gap between tranche 2 and kit revenue.
- [[Kit Tiers]] is a direct response to the "kits, not boards" argument, with the board-only tier as the concession to the people who will contribute.
- Model 4 is the trap. Tessa wrote "no consulting until the beta ships" on the whiteboard and it is still there.

## Doubts
The author sells thousands of units a year. Nothing in the series says whether the kit model works at 40 to 100 units with five people. That is the question the [[Community Launch]] will answer.
